# Burmese → Ukrainian practical transcription

## Purpose

This is the practical layer after the research pipeline:

**Burmese orthography → graphemic/syllabic analysis → Burmese phonology → IPA → Ukrainian target-language adaptation → Ukrainian practical orthography**

It is intended for personal names, geographical names, institutions, cultural/historical names and reference use. It is a project proposal, not an official Ukrainian standard.

## 1. Russian predecessor

A dedicated Russian practical tradition exists. V. G. Epstein's 1959 Rules of Russian Transcription of Burmese Geographical Names was published by the Institute of Oriental Studies of the USSR Academy of Sciences and was intended for Burmese geographical names, with application to personal names and other terms noted in the bibliographic record. A further 1978 institutional instruction for Russian transmission of Burmese geographical names is also documented, as is Epstein's 1958 article on transcription of Burmese proper names.

A modern documentary table preserves the same family of rules: positional consonants, medial rules, vowel/rime tables, final neutralization and non-marking of tone.

The Russian system is therefore a control system, not merely a random Cyrillic rendering.

Its strongest properties:
- onset/final distinction;
- explicit medials;
- checked-syllable treatment;
- nasal-final treatment;
- vowel/rime registry;
- tone outside ordinary practical spelling;
- special orthographic rules.

Its main limitation for Ukrainian is that it is optimized for Russian phonology and orthography.

## 3. Aspiration

Russian preserves /kʰ pʰ tʰ/ as кх пх тх. Ukrainian has no corresponding native aspirated-stop series, so the practical layer normally neutralizes aspiration:

- /k, kʰ/ → к
- /t, tʰ/ → т
- /p, pʰ/ → п
- /s, sʰ/ → с
- /tɕ, tɕʰ/ → ч where the Burmese phonological analysis establishes the affricate.

This does not claim aspiration is absent in Burmese.

## 4. /ŋ/

Russian нг is a useful precedent and is retained as the Ukrainian baseline. It exposes the velar nasal rather than collapsing it to н.

This is a project policy, not an official Ukrainian norm.

## 5. /w/

Russian documentary practice can use у for the consonantal glide. Ukrainian should not mechanically inherit this. The project baseline is в in consonantal position, while vocalic /u/ remains у.

This is a target-language optimization.

## 6. /h/

Russian х reflects Russian target-language phonology. Ukrainian distinguishes г /ɦ/ and х /x/. The project baseline is therefore г for foreign /h/, with х retained as an alternative candidate pending corpus adjudication.

## 7. /r/ and /j/

The Ukrainian layer must separate Burmese phonological /r/ from /j/. A Russian spelling convention cannot be used as evidence that the Burmese phoneme itself is /j/.

- Burmese /r/ → р
- Burmese /j/ → context-sensitive Ukrainian й / я / є / ю / йо

The actual orthographic choice belongs after IPA and target-language phonology.

## 9. Finals and checked syllables

Written Burmese finals are not copied mechanically. The practical layer distinguishes oral checked syllables, nasal finals, glide finals and orthographic markers.

Where the Burmese phonological analysis gives glottal closure rather than a released final consonant, the ordinary Ukrainian practical string should normally not invent a final consonant. Nasal finals may remain visible.

Pali/Sanskrit stacks are not allowed to enlarge the native Burmese phoneme inventory automatically.

## 10. Tone, register and phonation

The Russian practical system does not encode Burmese tone in the ordinary Cyrillic output. The Ukrainian project follows the same practical principle but keeps the analytical layer richer:

- practical mode: Ukrainian spelling only;
- research mode: Ukrainian spelling + Burmese tone/register/phonation metadata;
- IPA mode: full evidence-backed phonological/phonetic annotation.

Burmese tone must never be converted automatically into Ukrainian stress.

## 11. Ukrainian orthography

The target layer follows Ukrainian practical-transcription principles. The current Ukrainian state standard is the 2026 edition of Ukrainian Orthography, effective from 2026-03-28. Its normative status supports an independent Ukrainian target-language stage. The 2019 edition is retained only as historical provenance.

The system therefore does not import Russian ы, э, кх, тх, пх, or Russian-specific soft-sign conventions merely because they occur in the predecessor system.

## 13. Validation

Gold data should cover:
- geographical names;
- personal names;
- institutional names;
- cultural/historical names;
- common lexical items;
- checked syllables;
- nasal finals;
- medials;
- kinzi and stacks;
- Pali/Sanskrit-derived spellings;
- prosodic contrasts;
- established Ukrainian forms.

Report exact string match, syllable-level match, segment-level accuracy, unresolved rate and construction-specific error taxonomy with explicit denominators.

## 14. Central principle

The Russian system is a documented predecessor/control layer. The Ukrainian system becomes independently defensible when every divergence is explainable through:
1. Burmese phonology;
2. Burmese orthography;
3. Ukrainian phonology;
4. Ukrainian orthography;
5. evidence strength.
