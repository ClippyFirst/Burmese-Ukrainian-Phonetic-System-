# Burmese → Russian vs Ukrainian: comparative audit

## Executive finding

A dedicated Russian practical transcription tradition exists for Burmese. The 1959 Epstein rules are the principal historical Russian source located in this review. The documentary table shows a rich system of consonant-position, medial, vowel/rime and final rules.

The comparative architecture is:

Burmese form → Burmese phonology/IPA → Russian practical → Ukrainian candidate → difference → reason → evidence → status

Russian is not the source of truth for Ukrainian.

## What should be inherited

### Structural principles
- onset/coda distinction;
- medial parsing;
- checked-syllable treatment;
- nasal-final treatment;
- vowel/rime registration;
- tone outside ordinary practical spelling;
- explicit exceptional-orthography analysis.

### Not target-language rules
- кх/тх/пх;
- э;
- ы;
- /w/ → у;
- automatic х for /h/;
- Russian-specific soft-sign conventions;
- Russian spelling of Burmese /r/ where phonology is /r/.

## Why Ukrainian is independently useful

A second target-language solution allows empirical questions:

- Does нг maximize recoverability for /ŋ/?
- Is г or х preferable for Burmese /h/?
- Which /ɯ/ approximation is least misleading?
- How should /ɲ/ and /j/ sequences be rendered?
- Which Russian distinctions are target-language artifacts?
- Which Ukrainian candidates produce the most natural native reading?

These should be evaluated against a declared gold corpus.
